**Zhang Xunru** (July 1905— ), original name Zhang Qingze.
A native of Dongguang County, Hebei.

Zhang Xunru was originally a historical archivist who studied language and script as a hobby, later becoming a linguist.

In his youth he was admitted to the Second People's Night School Normal Class organized by Peking University, and later attended senior high school at Dongcheng Middle School. He was unable to graduate because the school closed, and transferred to an English institute for one year of study.

In 1925, through the introduction of Wei Jiangong, he took a position at the Committee for the Aftermath of the Qing Household.

In 1926, when the Palace Museum was founded, he took a post at its Archives Hall.

In 1929, he concurrently served as clerk at the [Chinese Great Dictionary Compilation Office]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link glossary/compilation.md %}), and in 1934 became a commissioned compiler.

In 1947, he served as National Language advisor at the Preparatory Class of Peking University.

In 1948, he returned to the Chinese Great Dictionary Compilation Office as a specially appointed compiler.

In December 1955, the Palace Museum Archives were transferred to the State Archives Bureau. Zhang Xunru successively served as group leader and associate researcher.

In 1959, he was transferred to the Archival Science Research Office of the History and Archives Department at Renmin University of China.

In August 1961, he was transferred back to the Central Archives, where he worked until retiring in 1971.
